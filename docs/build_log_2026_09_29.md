# Building a Trading Bot With Me: The First Paper Account

September 29, 2026 · Draft for review before publication

I'm building a trading bot in public. The first milestone is a working paper
account with a record of every decision, simulated fill, and cash balance.

For this first strategy, I'm using Meb Faber's monthly trend-following rule.
The model checks five ETF proxies: SPY for US stocks, EFA for developed foreign
stocks, IEF for Treasury bonds, VNQ for real estate, and GSG for commodities.
Each gets its own 20% allocation. At month-end, an ETF above its 10-month
moving average gets that allocation; below the average, its allocation goes
to cash. The calculation uses adjusted price levels, with raw prices used
for share sizing and simulated fills.

The system now fetches the data and produces current signals. A daily command
can process an eligible pending plan, update the account, and prepare the
next month's plan. A local dashboard shows holdings, cash, signals, pending
trades, and recorded account values. Running the command twice doesn't
duplicate trades.

The paper account opened today with $100,000. Its first plan has four pending
buys: SPY, EFA, VNQ, and GSG. IEF's allocation stays in cash. **No fills have
been recorded, so there is no trading performance to report yet.**

One detail I wanted to get right early: the system can't pretend it traded
at a price from before the plan existed. Tonight's latest fetched close was
September 28, while the plan was created September 29. The ledger correctly
left the plan waiting. Its first simulated fills must use a later completed
session.

The focused test suite has 48 passing tests, including date rules, repeat
runs, cash-shortfall rollback, cost basis, fees, slippage, and month changes.
For example, September's eligible fills must be processed before October's
new plan is created. Those future-date tests use synthetic prices; they
aren't market observations or performance results.

The next milestone is recording the first simulated fills and following the
account over time. I'll share the rules, results, mistakes, and fixes as the
project develops. The ledger currently needs manual entries for dividends
and interest, and it doesn't yet handle share-changing corporate actions.
Those limitations matter when evaluating its results.

This is a development and paper-trading project. No real broker orders are
submitted. The ETF version is a proxy implementation, and a working system
doesn't establish that the strategy will be profitable.

Rule reference: [Meb Faber, A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation](https://mebfaber.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/SSRN-id962461.pdf).
